How to choose a state for your LLC

Most guides list the filing fees and stop there. The fee is the smallest number in the decision — the annual upkeep and what the state makes public usually matter more.

Ohio$99$0None requiredOhio has no annual report at all for LLCs: $99 once, then nothing yearly to the Secretary of State. Do not confuse that with the Commercial Activity Tax, which is a separate revenue tax with its own threshold and its own filing.
Arizona$50$0None requiredArizona has no annual report for LLCs, so the $50 formation fee is the whole state bill. What the fee tables skip: some counties require publishing a notice in a local newspaper within a set window after formation.
Minnesota$155$0December 31 every yearMinnesota charges $155 to form and nothing to renew — one of very few states with a free annual renewal. The deadline is fixed at December 31, so it never lines up with your anniversary and is easy to miss even though it costs nothing.
Missouri$50$0None requiredMissouri has no annual report at all: $50 online and then nothing yearly to the state. Filing on paper instead of online costs $105 — the largest online-versus-paper gap in this database.
South Carolina$110$0None requiredSouth Carolina has no state annual report for LLCs: $110 once and nothing yearly to the Secretary of State. Do not confuse that with the county business license, which is separate, annual, and enforced locally.
Idaho$100$0Last day of the anniversary monthIdaho's annual report is free — but it is still mandatory. Skipping a $0 filing gets the LLC dissolved just as fast as skipping a paid one, which is the trap in every 'no annual fee' state.
Mississippi$50$0April 15 every yearMississippi's annual report costs nothing, which is exactly why it gets skipped. The deadline is April 15 and a missing $0 filing still ends in administrative dissolution.
Pennsylvania$125$7September 30 every yearThe annual report costs $7 — the cheapest in the country — but it is due September 30, not on your anniversary date. Miss it and the LLC stops being in good standing, which quietly blocks bank accounts and contracts.
New York$200$9Biennial, in the formation month; IT-204-LL due March 15The $9 biennial statement is the cheap part. New York also requires publishing the formation notice in two local newspapers for six weeks within 120 days, then filing a certificate of publication ($50). The newspaper bill runs from about $50 to well over $1,000 depending on the county — and almost no fee table mentions it.
Kentucky$40$15June 30 every year$40 to form and $15 a year makes Kentucky one of the cheapest states to keep alive. The deadline is June 30 for everyone, so it never matches your anniversary — and a missed report means the LLC is dissolved.
Hawaii$50$15Last day of the quarter containing the anniversaryThe $15 annual report is the cheapest part of operating in Hawaii. The General Excise Tax applies to gross revenue — not profit — from the first dollar, so the filing fee tells you almost nothing about the real cost.
Utah$59$18Last day of the anniversary monthUtah charges $59 to form and $18 a year to renew — among the lowest annual costs anywhere. The catch is that the renewal is easy to forget because it costs almost nothing, and the state does not chase you.

The registered agent is the part that decides whether the LLC survives; a stale address is the most common way these get dissolved.

The three that cover most people

Wyoming — the default for a non-resident who wants low cost and privacy. $100 to form, $60 a year, no state income tax, and member names are not public.

New Mexico — the cheapest LLC in the country to keep alive. $50 once and no annual report at all. The trade-off is a thinner support ecosystem: fewer registered agents advertise it.

Delaware — only worth it if you plan to raise money or bring on US investors. It costs $400 a year, does not file an annual report, and the March 1 deadline you see everywhere is for corporations, not LLCs.

States to think twice about

California charges an $800 minimum franchise tax every year, including the first one, before you earn anything. New York requires publishing your formation in two local newspapers, which can cost more than a thousand dollars depending on the county. Both are fine states if you actually operate there — just do not pick them for the fee.

What this guide does not tell you

It does not tell you where you owe income tax. That depends on where you live and where you work, not where you incorporate. A Wyoming LLC owned from abroad can still create tax obligations at home. Talk to an accountant in your own country before you file.
