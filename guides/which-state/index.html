How to choose a state for your LLC

Most guides list the filing fees and stop there. The fee is the smallest number in the decision — the annual upkeep and what the state makes public usually matter more.

South Carolina$110$0None requiredSouth Carolina has no state annual report for LLCs: $110 once and nothing yearly to the Secretary of State. Do not confuse that with the county business license, which is separate, annual, and enforced locally.
Pennsylvania$125$7September 30 every yearThe annual report costs $7 — the cheapest in the country — but it is due September 30, not on your anniversary date. Miss it and the LLC stops being in good standing, which quietly blocks bank accounts and contracts.
New York$200$9Biennial, in the formation month; IT-204-LL due March 15The $9 biennial statement is the cheap part. New York also requires publishing the formation notice in two local newspapers for six weeks within 120 days, then filing a certificate of publication ($50). The newspaper bill runs from about $50 to well over $1,000 depending on the county — and almost no fee table mentions it.
Utah$59$18Last day of the anniversary monthUtah charges $59 to form and $18 a year to renew — among the lowest annual costs anywhere. The catch is that the renewal is easy to forget because it costs almost nothing, and the state does not chase you.
Montana$35$20April 15 every yearAt $35 to form and $20 a year, Montana is one of the cheapest states in the country — which is exactly why it attracts owners who never set foot there. The registered agent is the part that decides whether the LLC survives; a stale address is the most common way these get dissolved.
Colorado$50$25Assigned report month (window opens 2 months before)The report month is assigned to the entity at formation — do not assume it matches your anniversary date; check the Summary page in the state's record. A late report carries a $50 penalty and flips the LLC to delinquent, then to administratively dissolved.
Michigan$50$25February 15 every yearThe annual statement is $25 and lands on February 15 for every LLC, no matter when you formed. It is cheap, which is exactly why people forget it — and Michigan dissolves companies over it.
Wisconsin$130$25End of the quarter you formed inWisconsin's annual report is $25, but the deadline follows the calendar quarter you formed in, not your exact date. A January LLC reports at the end of March, so the first one can arrive sooner than expected.
Oklahoma$100$25Last day of the anniversary monthOklahoma's annual certificate is $25, due by the last day of the anniversary month. The catch is the registered agent: Oklahoma requires one physically located in the state, which rules out most cheap mail-forwarding services.
Indiana$95$32End of the anniversary month, every second yearIndiana's filing is biennial, not annual: $32 every two years, due at the end of the anniversary month. Owners who set a yearly reminder end up filing twice as often as the state requires.
Georgia$100$50April 1 every yearGeorgia's annual registration is $50 and falls on April 1 for every LLC, no matter when you formed. Miss it and the state administratively dissolves the company — being late is not a fee, it is a status change.
Virginia$100$50Last day of the anniversary monthVirginia's annual registration fee is $50, due by the last day of the anniversary month. It is flat, not income-based, and the state charges it even in a year where the LLC did nothing at all.
Wyoming$100$60First day of the anniversary monthThe annual report is a license tax, not a flat fee: $60 or $0.0002 per dollar of Wyoming assets, whichever is greater. The $60 most guides quote is only the floor.
Washington$180$70Last day of the anniversary monthThe $180 is the online price; filing by mail costs $200. The annual report is $70 a year, and a late one costs $95. The B&O tax is assessed on gross receipts separately, so it can apply in a year with no profit at all.
Illinois$150$75First day of the anniversary monthIllinois charges $150 to form and $75 a year, and the annual report is due before the anniversary month begins — not at the end of it. Filing on the anniversary date is already late, which trips up almost every out-of-state owner.
New Jersey$125$75Last day of the anniversary monthNew Jersey's annual report is $75, due by the last day of the anniversary month. The state also carries one of the highest top income tax rates in the country, which matters if the LLC is a pass-through and the owner actually lives in New Jersey.
Florida$125$138.75May 1 every yearThe annual report is $138.75 and due May 1. Miss it and a $400 late fee is added with no waiver — Florida explicitly does not forgive this one. Keep missing it and the LLC is administratively dissolved by the third Friday of September.
North Carolina$125$200April 15 every yearNorth Carolina's annual report is $200 — more than the $125 it costs to form — and it is due April 15 whether or not the LLC earned anything. The report is cheap to ignore and expensive to miss.
Tennessee$300$300April 1 for calendar-year entitiesTennessee prices per member: $50 each with a $300 minimum, and the same formula applies every year to the annual report. A four-member LLC pays $300 to form and $300 a year before any tax — which is why it looks cheap in a one-column comparison and is not.
Maryland$100$300April 15 every yearMaryland's annual report is $300, three times the formation fee, and it is filed with the tax department rather than the business registry. Owners who only watch the Secretary of State never see the reminder.
Nevada$425$350Last day of the anniversary monthThe $425 up front is three things bundled together: $75 for the articles, $150 for the initial list, $200 for the state business license. The renewal is $350 a year, not $425, and missing it adds $175 in late fees.
Delaware$110$400June 1 every yearDelaware LLCs skip the annual report entirely and pay a flat $400 annual tax due June 1. Most comparison pages still show $300 (the old rate) and March 1 (the corporation deadline). Both are wrong for an LLC, and the late penalty is $200 plus 1.5% interest per month.
Massachusetts$500$500Anniversary dateMassachusetts charges $500 to form and $500 every year — the most expensive annual bill in this database. There is no cheap tier and no first-year relief, so the five-year cost is $2,500 before any tax.
California$70$800April 15 every year (first year: 15th day of the 4th month)The $70 is only the filing fee. California adds an $800 minimum franchise tax in the first year, a $20 statement of information every two years, and an extra LLC fee once California income reaches $250,000. The Secretary of State's own fee schedule lists the $70 and $20; the $800 comes from the tax agency, which is exactly why most comparison pages get the total wrong.

The three that cover most people

Wyoming — the default for a non-resident who wants low cost and privacy. $100 to form, $60 a year, no state income tax, and member names are not public.

New Mexico — the cheapest LLC in the country to keep alive. $50 once and no annual report at all. The trade-off is a thinner support ecosystem: fewer registered agents advertise it.

Delaware — only worth it if you plan to raise money or bring on US investors. It costs $400 a year, does not file an annual report, and the March 1 deadline you see everywhere is for corporations, not LLCs.

States to think twice about

California charges an $800 minimum franchise tax every year, including the first one, before you earn anything. New York requires publishing your formation in two local newspapers, which can cost more than a thousand dollars depending on the county. Both are fine states if you actually operate there — just do not pick them for the fee.

What this guide does not tell you

It does not tell you where you owe income tax. That depends on where you live and where you work, not where you incorporate. A Wyoming LLC owned from abroad can still create tax obligations at home. Talk to an accountant in your own country before you file.
